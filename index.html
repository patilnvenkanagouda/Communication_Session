01 · Definition

What Is Communication?

Communication is the process of sharing ideas and information between people. It can be written or spoken, verbal or non-verbal, pictorial or graphic.

It is a continuous process — and with today's technology, communication can be made simpler, faster, and more effective.

Why it matters
  • Good communication builds confidence. Poor communication leads to misunderstanding.
  • Engineers use communication to deliver information about their company, their products, and their ideas — to clients, teams, and employers.

04 · Barriers

Barriers to Effective Communication

Recognizing these barriers is the first step to overcoming them.

01

Physical Noise

Background sound, poor connections, or distance disrupt the message.

02

Language Gaps

Jargon, accents, or vocabulary differences cause confusion.

03

Perceptual Differences

People interpret the same message differently based on experience.

04

Emotional Barriers

Stress, anxiety, or mood can block a message from landing.

05

Cultural Differences

Norms around tone, directness, and gesture vary across cultures.

06

Information Overload

Too much detail at once causes key points to get lost.

Verbal Communication

Using spoken or written words to convey our message or ideas. Effective verbal communication builds good relationships with customers and helps improve business.

Where engineers use it

Six habits that make your spoken and written words land.

Voice Tone

Vary your tone — a flat tone bores the listener; the right tone keeps them attentive.

02

Listen carefully and avoid irritation — it prevents selfish decisions.

06 · Delivery

Making Verbal Communication Impactful

Small delivery habits that make a big difference when you speak.

Use Pauses
Replace filler words with a brief, confident pause. It gives you time to think and gives your words more weight.
Vary Your Volume
Speaking at different volumes keeps the listener engaged and highlights your key points.
Cut the Fillers
Swap "uh," "um," and "like" for silence — pauses sound far more confident than filler words.
Before
So, uh, the sensor, like, failed because, um, the voltage was, uh, too high.
After
The sensor failed — [pause] — because the voltage was too high.

Body Language & Non-Verbal Communication

The transmission of messages through eye contact, facial expressions, gestures, posture, and body language.

Facial Expression
Gestures
Eye Contact
Body Movement
Tips for non-verbal communication
  • Pay close attention — repeat back key points in the same words to show you care.
  • Maintain comfortable eye contact; fulfill their gaze without staring.
  • Keep an open body position — avoid crossing your arms.
  • Sit at the same level as the other person, not standing over them.
  • Sit angled toward the person rather than directly opposite — it feels less like an interview.
  • Avoid fidgeting — it can look uncomfortable, nervous, or bored.

07 · Visual

Visual Communication

Uses visual elements — animated GIFs, screenshots, videos, pie charts, infographics, and slide decks — to communicate ideas quickly.

EXAMPLES ON ENGINEERING PROJECTS
  • Circuit diagrams and CAD drawings
  • Flowcharts for processes or algorithms
  • Graphs and charts for test data
  • Screenshots and annotated photos for bug reports
Tips
  • Use colorful visuals — people respond to them
  • Use white space between images for a clean look
  • Learn when and how to use visuals effectively
A picture speaks fast
In a technical field, visuals often communicate complex data faster than paragraphs of text — a well-labeled chart can replace a page of explanation. Never let a visual replace clear labeling — a confusing chart is worse than none at all.

07 · Written

Written Communication

The sending of messages, orders, or instructions through letters, circulars, manuals, reports, memos, and bulletins — a formal, less-flexible channel.

EXAMPLES ON ENGINEERING PROJECTS
  • Project and lab reports
  • Emails to clients and vendors
  • Technical documentation and manuals
  • Meeting minutes and status memos
Tips
  • Keep the message clear and connect each sentence to the last
  • Keep your writing style simple and down to earth
  • Write legibly, or format cleanly, so it's easy to read
Written is permanent
A written record stays — it can be referenced, forwarded, and reviewed later, so accuracy and clarity matter even more. For a job application, written communication is often a client's or employer's first impression of you.

09 · Activity · Work in pairs

The 60-Second Pitch

Objective: practice clear delivery, confident pauses, and strong body language — together.

HOW TO RUN IT (WORK IN PAIRS)
1

Prepare · 1 min

Pick one idea, concept or any subject you know well. Jot down 2–3 key points — no full script.

2

Pitch · 60 sec

Explain it to your group as if they were a client. No "uh," "um," or "like" — use a pause instead.

3

Observe

Your group uses the checklist on the right while you speak, without interrupting.

4

Swap & Repeat

Switch roles and run it again — everyone pitches once.

5

Debrief · 2 min

Share one thing that felt hard and one thing your partner did well.

Observer Checklist
  • Filler words used (count them)
  • Pauses instead of fillers
  • Eye contact maintained
  • Open, relaxed body position
  • Tone changed for key points

In Summary

Conclusion

We need communication in every step of our lives — without it, life would not be possible. For growing good business and engineering skills, communication is essential.

  • Good communication skills build healthy relationships with customers and teams.
  • It helps grow business and increases profit.
  • Four types — verbal, non-verbal, visual, written — work together.
  • Small habits (tone, pauses, eye contact, clarity) create big impact.
  • Practice is what turns theory into a real skill — like today's pitch activity.
Communication is a continuous, two-way process. Practice it deliberately — in speech, body language, visuals, and writing.
